GRAND CANYON

A mile-deep hole in the ground. Official story: geology. Unofficial story: discuss among yourselves.

Field rule: Stay on marked trails and overlooks. Do not climb fences or wander onto unstable rim edges. Heat and dehydration are the real villains here.

The Grand Canyon is a National Park with real trails, real rim views and a real gift shop that will sell you a refrigerator magnet. Bring water, stay behind railings and mark every suspiciously perfect sunset. Geology did this. Probably.

Suspicious Spots

Public landmarks only. Stay where guests are welcome.

Mather Point (South Rim)

Classic first-look overlook near Grand Canyon Village. Crowded for a reason.

Yavapai Geology Museum

Rim views plus exhibits explaining those rock layers. Bring your "sure, Jan" face.

Desert View Watchtower

Mary Colter's 1932 tower on the East Rim Drive — views and architecture.

Hermit's Rest

West end of Hermit Road; stone building, snacks and a less-packed vibe off-peak.

Grand Canyon Visitor Center

Orientation, maps and the shuttle hub. Start here so you don't invent your own trail.

Agent Notes

  • South Rim is open year-round; North Rim has a shorter season. Check NPS alerts before you go.
  • The canyon is about a mile deep and up to 18 miles wide. Your phone battery will not survive both rims in one day.
  • Leave rocks, fossils and artifacts where you found them. The park is not a loot crate.
  • If your theory involves ancient astronaut landscaping, keep it in the group chat — and stay on the trail.

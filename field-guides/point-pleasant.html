POINT PLEASANT, WV (MOTHMAN)

Red eyes. Silver Bridge memory. World's only Mothman Museum. Bring binoculars.

Field rule: TNT Area / McClintic WMA: stay on legal trails, heed hunting-season rules and don't enter unstable structures. Downtown is strollable and statue-friendly.

In November 1966, witnesses near the WWII TNT area north of Point Pleasant described a winged, red-eyed figure. Sightings piled up; the Silver Bridge collapse in December 1967 sealed the town into American weird-history. Today: a museum, a 12-foot statue, floodwall murals and public trails through the old ordnance works. Pay respects at the bridge memorial — that part isn't a bit.

Suspicious Spots

Public landmarks only. Stay where guests are welcome.

Mothman Museum

400 Main St. Archives, film props, souvenirs. World's only — they checked.

Mothman Statue (Gunn Park / Main St)

12-ft stainless steel by Bob Roach. The required selfie.

Silver Bridge Memorial

Riverfront remembrance of the 46 lives lost Dec 15, 1967. Be decent.

McClintic Wildlife Management Area (TNT Area)

~5 miles north. Hiking among WWII ordnance-works remnants; first sighting lore lives here. Public WMA rules apply.

Riverfront Park & floodwall murals

Ohio River views, history murals and easy downtown walking.

Agent Notes

  • John Keel's book and the later film amplified the legend; the museum collects the folklore layer on top of real tragedy and real sightings reports.
  • Mothman Festival hits downtown each September — book rooms early if that's your infiltration window.
  • Tu-Endie-Wei State Park marks the Battle of Point Pleasant (1774) — history stacked on history.
  • Cryptid tourism funds small-town Main Street. Buy coffee. Don't litter the TNT trails.

BERMUDA TRIANGLE (MIAMI / KEY WEST JUMP-OFF)

Start in South Florida. Point your boat at myth. Return with sunscreen.

Field rule: Ocean travel is serious. Use licensed operators, check weather, wear life jackets and file float plans. This guide is entertainment, not a passage plan.

Download printable

The Bermuda Triangle is a pop-culture wedge of Atlantic roughly between Miami, Bermuda and Puerto Rico. You cannot buy a ticket to the Triangle itself — you launch from real ports. Miami and Key West are the classic U.S. jump-offs: piers, reefs, sunset sails and a century of disappearances that look less spooky once you add "weather" and "old navigation" to the spreadsheet. Still fun to mark the vibes.

Suspicious Spots

Public landmarks only. Stay where guests are welcome.

Miami / Biscayne Bay waterfront

Public baywalks, piers and licensed boat tours — your mainland staging area.

Key West Mallory Square & harbor

Sunset celebration energy; ferries and charters for legitimate on-water time.

Southernmost Point Buoy (Key West)

Photo op chaos. Early morning = fewer elbows.

Dry Tortugas day trip (ferry / seaplane)

Fort Jefferson out in the Gulf — remote enough to feel Triangle-adjacent without the myth tax.

Nova Southeastern / maritime museums (Miami area)

When you want charts and history instead of vibes.

Agent Notes

  • Lloyd's and insurers have long argued the region's loss rates aren't uniquely supernatural once traffic volume is counted — boring, but peer-reviewable.
  • Gulf Stream currents and sudden weather explain more than portals. Portals are more Instagrammable.
  • If you charter, pick Coast Guard–licensed operators. Ghost stories don't inflate life jackets.
  • Puerto Rico and Bermuda are the other corners — this U.S. jump-off guide keeps the packing list shorter.

This Week in Tinfoil

Posted 10/30/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

Every week the conspiracy machine finds a new place to plug in its corkboard. This week it skipped the usual suspects and went straight for college football, stock car racing, election offices and the cruising altitude of a passenger jet. Grab your red yarn. Label every pin UNCONFIRMED.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The kickoff-time cover-up

LSU coach Lane Kiffin learned his Nov. 7 home game against Alabama will kick off at 2:30 p.m. Central instead of under the lights, and he did what any red-blooded American does when a schedule disappoints him: he floated a theory. Per AL.com and CBS Sports, he said he was joking that the SEC wanted to take away LSU's night-game edge, and that anyone who thinks the league wants LSU to win "must've just been on Mars the last four months."

Notice the technique. First comes "people are saying." Then the speaker quietly joins the people. That's not a football move; that's a campaign-trail move, and politicians of every party have been running it for decades. The boring counterpoint, also in the AL.com piece: the TV partner sets kickoff times, and a Miami vs. Notre Dame game already owns the primetime slot. Our ruling is that we don't do rulings. We do, however, award Kiffin one honorary tin foil whistle.

Exhibit B: Talladega and the telemetry

Kyle Larson went from 11th to 1st on the final lap at Talladega, and within minutes the internet had two theories ready: he dipped below the yellow line and NASCAR looked away, or his fellow Chevy drivers lifted off the gas to wave him through. According to this AOL report, NASCAR said its telemetry showed the trailing drivers stayed at full throttle, and Hendrick Motorsports president Jeff Andrews laughed the whole thing off on SiriusXM.

Here's what makes this one a skeptic's dream: there's actual data. Fans who think something's off can ask what the telemetry does and doesn't show, who audits it, and why the yellow-line rule is a judgment call in the first place. That's how you dig in. "My cousin's buddy saw it on a livestream" is not how you dig in.

Exhibit C: Why Maricopa County never gets a day off

Every election season, one Arizona county ends up as the main character. Fast Company dug into why Maricopa keeps landing at the center of election theories. Read it yourself and decide what you think.

Our equal-opportunity roast: one side treats every slow count like a heist movie, and the other side treats every question like treason. Both sides fundraise off the drama, and both send you an email with "URGENT" in the subject line by Tuesday. The skeptic move is the unglamorous one: look up how the county actually counts, watch who's observing, and notice which headlines are written to make you angry rather than informed.

Exhibit D: A pill in the sky

And for dessert, pilots reportedly photographed a pill-shaped object while flying at 41,000 feet over Ohio (BroBible has the photo story), while across the pond outlets are mapping UK sightings from Pentagon-released files. We have questions. Is it a balloon? A drone? A very ambitious vitamin? The beauty of a released file is that you can read it instead of trusting a guy on a podcast or a guy at a podium.

The pattern on the corkboard

Connect the dots across all four exhibits and you get the real lesson of the week. A coach, a race team, an election office and a cockpit window all ran into the same thing: when people don't trust the referee, they invent one. The fix isn't believing everything or believing nothing. It's asking for the record, reading it and arguing about it with your friends over snacks.

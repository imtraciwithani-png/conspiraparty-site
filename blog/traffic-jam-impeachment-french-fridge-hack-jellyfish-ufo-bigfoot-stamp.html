Gridlock, Hot Fridges and a Sky Jellyfish

Posted 11/11/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

It's Wednesday, and the Agency's corkboard has a theme nobody planned: things that showed up late, things that got too warm, and things that refuse to be identified. A traffic jam decided a vote in Congress. Somebody turned France's supermarket fridges into ovens. A jellyfish went flying over Alaska. And Bigfoot finally got the government recognition he deserves: a stamp. Grab your red string.

Exhibit A: The impeachment that got stuck in traffic

On Tuesday the House voted 211-204 to send eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, which effectively parks them, per The Washington Sun. Eleven Democrats missed the vote, and if all of them had shown up and voted no, the motion would have failed. Several said they were coming back from an event about a mile and a half away when, in the words of Rep. Jonathan Jackson, "a traffic incident and a police-escorted bus caused a delay," The Independent reports. Rep. Marilyn Strickland's office says she was stuck behind a motorcade and missed it by one minute. Others cited a medical procedure, a personal emergency and a veterans event back home, per Al Jazeera.

The Agency would like to roast everyone in the building equally. To the party that spent months building a case and then lost to a bus: you had one job, and it was "be in the room." Even Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez pointed out she used to have to clock in, per Scripps News. To the majority: a Democratic aide argued Republicans gaveled the vote closed knowing members were on the way, which is the legislative version of locking the door when you see your roommate pull into the driveway. And to the conspiracy crowd already drafting "the motorcade was a psy-op" threads: the most powerful force in Washington isn't the deep state. It's D.C. traffic. It has no ideology. It hates everyone.

Exhibit B: The great French fridge heist

France officially blamed Iran's Revolutionary Guard for an October cyberattack that hit refrigeration controls at about 700 supermarket sites, reports the AP. According to the French government's official statement, the attackers set defrost settings to high temperatures, spoiling tens of millions of euros of food and sparking fires at some sites. The prosecutor told AFP the hackers got in through web interfaces and kicked off "abnormal, indefinite defrost cycles." France summoned Iran's ambassador and wants EU sanctions.

This is serious sabotage, and it also proves the thing your uncle has been yelling at Thanksgiving for years: the fridge is connected to the internet and it should not be. Somewhere a smart-appliance marketing team is quietly deleting the slide that says "control your cold storage from anywhere!" The skeptic lesson writes itself. The scariest hacks aren't movie-style hackers in hoodies breaking into missile codes. It's a default password on a box that keeps the brie cold. Change your passwords, people. The cheese is counting on you.

Exhibit C: A jellyfish over Anchorage

A visitor named Sara Jane Osborn says she was photographing a rainbow in Anchorage back on June 15 when a dark, black-and-silver shape with dangling "tentacles" hovered over the trees for about three minutes and then vanished, per IBTimes UK. The couple thought it was in the direction of Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson. The video went viral this week. Even the outlets running it note the location, size and identity are unverified, and Osborn herself said "maybe it's a drone-type thing," per WION.

Believers: "It's a living craft from beneath the ice!" Skeptics: "It's a kite, Brenda." Reddit, naturally, is doing both at once. The Agency's official position is that if a giant jellyfish is in fact patrolling Alaskan airspace, it picked the right state, because nobody up there is going to be surprised by anything. Also, waiting five months to post your UFO video is the most Alaska thing that has ever happened.

Exhibit D: Bigfoot gets a government job

The U.S. Postal Service announced its 2027 stamps, and the lineup includes a "Mythical Creatures" set: Sasquatch, Mothman, the Jackalope, Champ the Lake Champlain monster and the Squonk, a Pennsylvania legend that cries all the time, per the USPS newsroom. The art is tattoo-style, drawn by artist Nat Power. USPS itself says the Squonk is "today a beloved symbol of anxiety," which makes it the official stamp of anyone who has ever opened a credit card statement.

Think about what this means. The federal government, which officially has no evidence of Bigfoot, will now print his face and sell it to you for the price of a first-class letter. That's not disclosure. That's licensing. Mothman went from license plates to postage, and at this rate he'll be on the twenty-dollar bill by 2030.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's theme: the boring explanation is usually the right one, and it's usually worse. The vote wasn't stolen by a shadowy plot, it was lost to a bus. The fridges didn't turn on their owners, somebody left the door unlocked online. The sky jellyfish might be a kite. And the creature the government denies exists is now sticking to your mail. Question everything, especially traffic.

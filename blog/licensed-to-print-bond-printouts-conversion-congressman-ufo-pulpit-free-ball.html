Licensed to Print: The 007 Printouts

Posted 11/25/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

Thanksgiving Eve’s file is about vetting. The White House checked out the new James Bond the old-fashioned way: on paper. A congressman-elect announced a mission to convert an entire demographic, then clarified something nobody asked. A temple in Thailand put its monk in a flying saucer. And cricket invented a way to get out without swinging. Grab the red string and a dinner roll.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: Licensed to print

Per the New York Post, which had the interview, and The Hill, President Trump was asked Tuesday about Robert Pattinson being cast as the next 007. He called through the open Oval Office door, “Is James Bond conservative? Natalie.” Aide Natalie Harp came in with printouts and said, “He’s been good.” Trump said, “I always like people that like me,” and wished Pattinson luck. One of the printouts was his own 2012 tweet telling Pattinson not to take back Kristen Stewart. His review now: “That was a long time ago.” The Independent notes it wasn’t clear what nice things Pattinson had actually said.

We love this for the intelligence community. Forget MI6. The most powerful vetting operation on Earth is one person sprinting in from the outer office with a stack of paper. The real twist is the 2012 tweet. Imagine your old relationship advice to a vampire actor being handed back to you as a classified briefing. Fair to everyone: every politician vets celebrities by whether they’ve been nice to them. This one just has a printer.

Exhibit B: The conversion congressman

Newsweek and The Independent report that Anthony Constantino, the newly elected Republican taking over Elise Stefanik’s New York seat and a part-time rapper, said one of his top priorities is to “get every gay American to be Republican.” He then posted screenshots of flirty DMs as proof it was working, adding, “I want to clarify, I am not Gay.” He also sang some Stevie Wonder in his car. Per LGBTQ Nation, nobody knows who sent those messages or whether they were jokes.

Skeptic’s note: “some anonymous DMs” is the exact evidence standard of every UFO forum we’ve ever lurked on. Screenshots with the names blacked out, a man insisting it’s working, and a pinned post. That’s not a campaign, that’s a Bigfoot sighting with a ring light. Democrats get no free pass either. Their side answered with exit-poll math and a press statement, which is like answering a kazoo solo with a spreadsheet. Nobody ever won over a voter by being the most boring person in the room.

Exhibit C: The UFO pulpit

The Bangkok Post and MGR Online report that Wat Bandai Thong in Phetchaburi held its Nov. 23–24 Mahachat sermon with a monk preaching from a replica spaceship. The temple’s pre-event post: “This year we are going to travel through outer space together — but don’t be alarmed just yet.” Its point was that a spaceship is just a vehicle humans built, while the teachings are the real trip.

Finally, somebody handled UFO disclosure correctly. No hearings, no tranches, no whistleblower on a podcast. Just a monk, a glowing saucer and a calm explanation that it’s a metaphor. Transparency. We’re impressed.

Exhibit D: The free ball

BBC Sport and Cricinfo report that in the Women’s Big Bash League, Melbourne Renegades batter Deandra Dottin took longer than the 75-second limit to get ready, so she had to stand aside while Brisbane Heat’s Sarah Glenn bowled a “free ball” at the empty stumps. Glenn hit them. Out, golden duck, first time in Big Bash history. The Renegades still won off the last ball.

This is the most honest rule in sports. Be late, and the universe gets a free shot at you. We’d like it adopted for Thanksgiving: show up after the turkey’s carved and Aunt Linda gets one free question about your love life, no defense allowed.

The pattern on the corkboard

Everybody today was vetting by vibes. A president checked Bond’s politics by printout. A congressman proved his plan with anonymous DMs. A temple vetted the saucer and told you it was fake up front, which makes it the most trustworthy UFO of 2026. And cricket proved the clock doesn’t care about your excuses. Check the source, check the screenshots and get to the crease on time.

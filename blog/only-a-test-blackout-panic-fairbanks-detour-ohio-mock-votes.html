Only a Test, Please Remain Calm

Posted 11/17/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

Tuesday's theme at the Agency is simple: things that were only a test, only the weather, or only a practice run. The internet treated all three like the season finale. Grab the red string. We're going to need less of it than people think.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The blackout that was a beep

FEMA and the FCC scheduled a nationwide Emergency Alert System test for Tuesday, Nov. 17, at about 2:20 p.m. Eastern (12:20 p.m. Mountain). It lasts about a minute, airs on TV and radio only, and your phone won't buzz, per the Cortez Current. Federal law requires one at least every three years. FEMA's own summary of the panic: "No blackouts, no secret emergencies," per WXII.

Meanwhile, TikTok decided the beep was the starting gun for a 90-day nationwide blackout. PolitiFact found one viral clip that edited a Nov. 9 Trump press appearance to make him "announce" the blackout. He didn't. The account behind it used the fake doom to push crypto. Nothing says "the grid is about to collapse" like "quick, buy an internet coin." PolitiFact also notes that past alert tests were blamed for turning people into zombies. So this year's rumor is actually a downgrade. No zombies. Just a sale pitch.

Exhibit B: The Fairbanks detour

On Monday, the Qatar-gifted Air Force One taking President Trump to Asia refueled in Fairbanks instead of Anchorage. The White House said it was weather, and the National Weather Service was forecasting freezing rain around Anchorage, per The Daily Beast. The older blue 747 parked nearby, which the White House says is a support plane carrying staff and gear. MEAWW's fact check rates the online "security threat" claims false.

Now let's roast everybody fairly. One headline called a weather reroute a "bizarre new glitch," which is a bold way to describe sleet. Online sleuths saw two planes and wrote a spy movie. And the White House asked the press pool to hold the news of the change before calling the coverage "fear-mongering slop," per the Daily Beast. Nothing calms conspiracy theorists like asking reporters to keep a secret about the weather. Everyone did their part to turn a gas stop into a season finale.

Exhibit C: Ohio's ghost voters were practice voters

In Wyandot County, Ohio, the unofficial Election Night report showed 8,739 ballots cast, but 9,855 votes in the governor's race. Posts called it proof of fraud. The county's explanation: totals from an Oct. 28 mock election were accidentally left in the system for one precinct, and no outcome changed, per WTOL. The elections director told Lead Stories: "Those numbers do not math." The bipartisan canvass began Nov. 16, and certification is due by Nov. 24.

The Agency respects a public official who admits the numbers don't math. That's more honesty than most group chats manage. The fraud crowd was right that something was off. They were just wrong about the villain. It wasn't a cabal. It was a practice run nobody cleared out, like a dry-erase board with last week's lunch order still on it. And the system that caught it is the boring one: unofficial results, then a canvass, then certification. Boring is how you know it's working.

The pattern on the corkboard

Every one of today's files started with a real, checkable detail: a scheduled test, a weather forecast, a spreadsheet that didn't add up. Then the internet skipped the checking part and went straight to the trailer voice. The Agency's rule for this week: if the official explanation is "it was only a test," read the test schedule before you buy the generator. Then laugh.

After the Fact: The Giant Hoax at the Pump

Posted 11/23/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

Monday's file is about timing. Gas got cheaper after an election, so it must be a plot. A college is finally offering credit for staring at the sky. New Yorkers held a spy lookalike contest in broad daylight. A woman waited 33 years to mention some red lights. And astronomers think a planet might be hiding out past Neptune, because of course it is. Grab the red string.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The Giant Hoax at the pump

Per Talking Points Memo, President Trump posted on Truth Social Sunday: "Isn't it interesting that Oil prices have dropped precipitously after the Midterm Elections?" He added, "The whole thing is a Giant Hoax!" Yahoo Finance reports he offered no evidence, and that prices had already started sliding before Election Day, with analysts pointing at supply and shipping.

Let's be fair to everyone here, which is to say, unfair to everyone. When gas goes up, the party in power blames the other guys. When gas goes down, the party in power takes a bow. This is the first time we've seen a guy get mad that gas got cheaper. That's a new move in the spin playbook, and we respect the creativity. Meanwhile, the opposition will spend the week explaining that cheap gas is actually their accomplishment. Nobody, at any point, will credit the oil market for doing oil market things. The economy is the only conspiracy where both sides claim to be the mastermind.

Exhibit B: UFO 101, now for credit

According to a press release from the Society for UAP Studies, Elizabethtown College in Pennsylvania is opening registration for "Intro to UAP Studies," PH270, one of the first college-credit courses on unidentified anomalous phenomena. It's online, it starts in Spring 2027, and the course page lists it as three credits for $499.

For years your uncle said he'd been "doing his own research." Now he can get a transcript. We have questions for the syllabus committee. Is the final exam open-sky? Does a blurry phone video count as a citation? Is there extra credit for a weather balloon you correctly identified as a weather balloon? Honestly, this is the healthiest thing in the whole file. Put the subject in a classroom, teach people how to weigh evidence, and half the internet's UFO arguments end by midterms.

Exhibit C: The spy lookalike contest

India Today and Moneycontrol report that New Yorkers held a "Chinese Spy Lookalike Contest" in Washington Square Park, riffing on a viral online controversy. Contestants dressed up, acted out handing off "classified documents," and the crowd voted by QR code. The prize was $50.

Fifty dollars. Real spies get paid in briefcases. This contest paid in one nice dinner, no appetizer. And think about the logic: if you're actually a spy, the one thing you don't do is win a spy lookalike contest. Second place is the real agent. Third place is the guy filming it for TikTok, who is also, statistically, a spy. Our field office has added "lookalike contests" to the list of things New York does better than anyone, right behind pizza and complaining.

Exhibit D: The 33-year silence

Per North Wales Live, Alexandra Newby says that in September 1993, while staying in her family's caravan near Valle Crucis Abbey in Llangollen, she saw silent red lights in a V shape gliding over the hills. She kept it to herself for more than three decades because she thought nobody would believe her.

Thirty-three years. Most of us can't keep a group-chat secret for thirty-three minutes. We're not here to say what she saw. Neither is she, really, and that's the honest part. But the timing is perfect: you wait three decades, and the week you finally speak up, there's a college course you can take about it. That's not a coincidence. That's a marketing department. (It's a coincidence.)

Bonus exhibit: Planet X2

ScienceAlert covers a study in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society suggesting an Earth-class world might orbit hundreds of times farther from the Sun than we are. Nobody has actually seen it. The math just says something out there is nudging the icy rocks.

So to recap, the solar system may have a planet that hides, won't show its face and is only known by its effect on others. That's not a planet. That's a lobbyist.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's theme is "after." Prices dropped after the election. A course launched after decades of whispering. A contest popped up after a viral story. A woman spoke up after 33 years. "It happened after, so it's connected" is the oldest trick in the conspiracy book, and every side of the aisle reaches for it. Sometimes things are connected. Usually they just happened in order. Check the sources, ask the dumb questions, and question everybody, especially us.

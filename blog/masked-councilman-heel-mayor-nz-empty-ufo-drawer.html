Masks On, Memos Off

Posted 11/10/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

It's Tuesday, and the Agency's corkboard has accidentally turned into a wrestling card. In one corner, a masked luchador-style councilman who just won the right to legislate in full costume. In the other, a wrestler who played a villainous politician until voters made him a real one. On the undercard: a military that searched 16 years of email for the word "UFO" and found nothing, and two horses who tried public transit. Ring the bell.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The man in the golden mask (is on the zoning committee)

In Kawanishi, Japan, pro wrestler Billyken Kid topped the city council election in October, campaigning in his mask and pulling 4,040 votes, the most of any of 37 candidates, per Dexerto. On Nov. 6, four of the council's five groups voted to let him keep it on in the chamber, The Mainichi reports. The conditions: it has to be the same mask from his election posters, he has to keep order, and the chair checks his real face before he walks in so nobody can sneak in wearing a spare.

The Agency wants to point out that this is the single most transparent secret identity in the history of government. He shows his face to the chairman, then puts on the exact mask the voters approved. That is more identity verification than most of us went through to open a streaming account. The one group that objected argued he'd appeared unmasked at other events, which is a fair point about consistency and also the plot of every superhero movie. Slam Wrestling notes a different masked councilman in Oita lost this same fight back in 2021, so this is officially a rematch.

Exhibit B: The heel turn that went the other way

Meanwhile in Rhode Island, state representative David Morales was elected mayor of Providence with about 81% of the vote, according to SEScoops and KSBW. At 28 he'll be the city's youngest mayor, and in his spare time he has wrestled for the Renegade Wrestling Alliance as "The Commissioner," a villainous politician character. Critics in the primary mailed out pictures of him in his singlet calling him "all hype, no results," the Boston Globe reports, and it did not work.

Let's be clear: the villain is the character. That's the whole joke, and it's a good one. Every politician on earth is accused of playing a part. This one just admitted it, had a costume and a championship belt, and sold tickets. His supporters will call it authenticity. His critics will call it a gimmick. Wrestling fans will call it a face turn. Asked if he'll keep wrestling, he said "never say never." The Agency respects a man who leaves a door open for a steel chair at the budget hearing.

Exhibit C: The world's emptiest UFO file

Every week someone insists the files are being hidden. This week New Zealand's Defence Force answered an Official Information Act request by searching all its correspondence from January 2010 to June 2026 for "UFO," "UAP" and the long versions of both. Result, in the official words: "nil results," per RNZ. The NZDF says it simply doesn't handle UFO reports anymore. Its 15 volumes of older sightings from 1952 to 2009 went to public libraries in 2010, and the signed response says its own copies were then destroyed.

Now watch both teams spin it. Believers: "Zero emails in 16 years? Nobody is that quiet. They moved it to the secret server." Skeptics: "Zero emails because there was nothing to email about." The boring truth on paper is that they hand reports to aviation officials and don't keep a UFO desk. The funniest truth is that the most complete UFO archive in New Zealand is sitting in a public library in Hamilton, available with a library card. The cover-up has late fees.

Exhibit D: Odd-news side quest

In Detroit, a driver filmed two horses boarding a city bus on Seven Mile Road on Saturday, and CBS Detroit checked the metadata and says it's real, not AI. The horses reportedly balked at the wheelchair ramp, hopped on anyway, and exited out the back almost immediately. The transit system's rules allow only service animals that fit on a lap or the floor. Neither horse qualified. Both looked like they'd have opinions on the route map.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's theme is costumes and paperwork. A masked man who proved who he is. A villain who was only acting. A drawer that's empty because the files were given away in public. The skeptic move is the same as always: before you decide what's hidden, check what's already been handed to you. Sometimes the secret identity is on the campaign poster.

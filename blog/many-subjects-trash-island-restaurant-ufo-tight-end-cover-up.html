Meetings on Many Subjects, Details Classified

Posted 11/16/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

It's Monday, and the Agency's corkboard has a new header card: MANY SUBJECTS. Nobody knows what's under it. That's the point. This week's files include a mystery agenda, a golf course with a villain name, a UFO swarm that was hanging from a restaurant ceiling, and football fans investigating their own team. Grab the red string. Bring snacks.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The agenda that launched a thousand theories

At 8:42 a.m. Sunday, President Trump posted on Truth Social that he was in the White House "having meetings on many subjects," and called the U.S. the "hottest" country in the world, per IBTimes UK. The internet immediately compared it to someone wiggling a mouse so Microsoft Teams keeps them green. The public schedule listed "Executive Time" at 8 a.m., and IBTimes fairly notes that doesn't prove no meetings happened. It just doesn't say what they were.

So we have two teams of investigators. Team One says "many subjects" is code for "nothing." Team Two says "many subjects" is code for "everything." Both teams are building theories on four words and a calendar entry. The Agency's ruling: "many subjects" is the most honest status update any boss has ever posted. Every office worker in America has written that exact sentence while eating a granola bar.

Exhibit B: The Battle of Trash Island

A judge has delayed, for another two weeks, the Interior Department's renovation of East Potomac Golf Links in D.C., and Trump says the government will appeal on an expedited basis, per Just The News. His post calls it "Trash Island," adds "They don't call it Trash Island for no reason!" and warns that "golf seasons, jobs, prestige, and much money, will be lost!"

Let's admire the bipartisan comedy here. One side is treating a public golf course like a national emergency. The other side is treating a golf course renovation like a constitutional crisis. Meanwhile "Trash Island" is the best supervillain lair name Washington has produced in years. If a golf course can get an expedited appeal, the rest of us would like one for the DMV line.

Exhibit C: The Cheyenne swarm was inside the building

K2 Radio's Glenn Woods reported a "herd" of UFOs over Cheyenne while eating at a local restaurant. Then came the twist: the lights were "coming from INSIDE THE RESTAURANT." The ceiling fixtures were reflecting in the window, and the double-pane glass doubled them, per K2 Radio. He also notes the National UFO Reporting Center lists 421 Wyoming reports, and delivers the skeptic's golden rule: "Just because you don't know what it is does not mean it comes from outer space."

The Agency salutes this man for debunking his own sighting before dessert. That's more self-awareness than most cable panels manage in a whole season. He also admits he wants aliens to visit but doesn't want to be probed, which is the most relatable position in all of ufology.

Exhibit D: Somebody finally wants better evidence

The Scientific Coalition for UAP Studies launched a $100,000 campaign for six solar-powered camera stations that record the night sky on their own for weeks. When two or more stations catch the same object, they can work out its position, speed and acceleration. The hardware and software will be open source, and field trials are planned for Massachusetts in July 2027 and New Mexico after September 2027, per EIN Presswire.

That's the anti-conspiracy, honestly. No blurry phone clips and no "trust me, bro." Just two cameras, some math and data anyone can check. Believers get real measurements. Skeptics get real measurements. And somewhere a restaurant chandelier gets nervous.

Exhibit E: The tight end cover-up

Detroit Lions fans have a theory: tight end Sam LaPorta is being "punished" with fewer passes after his frank contract comments on a podcast in October, per SideLion Report, which says the theory doesn't hold up. He ran a route on 29 of 30 pass plays he was on the field for, and the bad stretch lines up with tough matchups. Sunday he caught one of four targets for 16 yards in a 35-10 win over the Patriots in Munich, per CBS Sports.

When your team wins 35-10 and you still find a cover-up, you're not a fan anymore. You're an agent. Welcome aboard.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's lesson: vague information is conspiracy fuel. "Many subjects," a blurry light, a quiet stat line. Each one is a blank space, and people fill it with whatever they already believed. The fix is boring and beautiful: more cameras, better data, and checking whether the UFO is a light fixture. Then laugh.

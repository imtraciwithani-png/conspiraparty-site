File Dump Friday

Posted 11/20/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

Friday is the government's favorite day to dump paperwork, and today the paperwork came in three flavors: hairy, glowing and missing. The Forest Service opened its Bigfoot drawer. The Pentagon posted UFO tranche number eight. A congressman sent letters about government chats that may have deleted themselves. And over in London, a baroness sang. Grab the red string and a highlighter.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The Forest Service opens the Bigfoot folder

After decades of official silence (plus the odd cheeky social post), the U.S. Forest Service has started treating Bigfoot like paperwork. Per The Washington Post, the agency quietly posted historical "Bigfoot/Sasquatch Records" to its FOIA reading room, with accounts from Montana and Washington going back to 1941, and even hosted a Bigfoot forum in a California national forest.

Believers are thrilled. Then they read the file. According to KING 5, the star document is a 1982 investigation of giant prints in Washington's Mill Creek watershed. A wildlife biologist noted the team's boots sank deeper into the mud than the "15-inch" prints did. The stride stayed about four feet whether the trail was gentle or steep. A U.S. Border Patrol tracker wrote that he thought someone made the prints with a foot-shaped device, since the tracks appeared and vanished with no trail leading in or out. Also, an elk stepped on one. Nobody interviewed the elk.

The Agency's verdict: the government finally released the Bigfoot files, and the files say "probably a guy with a fake foot." That's the most honest a declassified document has ever been. Believers get a federal folder with Bigfoot's name on it. Skeptics get a 1982 memo that does their homework for them. Everybody wins except the guy with the plaster foot, who has been anonymous for 44 years and should keep it that way.

Exhibit B: Tranche eight and the glowing dot

Meanwhile, the Department of War posted its eighth release of UAP files today at war.gov/UFO. The headline clip, per The Independent, is 20 seconds of grainy infrared footage from a U.S. warship in the Middle East in 2020, showing a circular light passing from right to left. A Pentagon official said it is not providing any comment or assessment "so that the American people can make up their own minds."

That's a bold strategy: release the blurry video, withhold the opinion, and let the group chat do the analysis. The same batch includes a 1953 report by Lockheed legend Kelly Johnson describing a dark blue "saucer," with his own sketches. The Daily Mail says it also includes a contractor report on Skinwalker Ranch describing plans for an underground vault for "exotic material or tissue." Plans for a vault are not a vault, and a vault is not a body. We checked our own corkboard. Still just pushpins.

The fair roast: believers will call a dot proof, debunkers will call a dot nothing, and the Pentagon's official position is "you tell us." The physicist who used to run its UFO office has said these videos tend to have mundane explanations. Eight tranches in, the most consistent finding is that infrared cameras love a warm smudge.

Exhibit C: The files nobody can release

Here's the twist on transparency week. Per The Washington Post, Rep. Jamie Raskin, poised to lead House Judiciary, sent letters to federal law enforcement agencies telling them to preserve chats on outside messaging apps and to try to recover records that were improperly deleted. His letter says there is "significant evidence" officials destroyed government records. That's his allegation, and the agencies get to answer it.

Spin check on both benches: the administration will be releasing 1953 saucer sketches while being asked where the old group chats went. And Congress will be demanding records preservation right before a "flurry of investigations," which is the political version of announcing a surprise party. If anything is truly lost, the only agency with a 100% recovery rate is your aunt's Facebook memories.

Exhibit D: The Lords' secret anthem

For dessert, The Spectator caught Labour's Baroness Chapman welcoming new peer June Sarpong by singing Elton John's rewritten "Your Song" line: "I can tell everybody, I am June Sarpong." The debate, per the transcript, was about sanitary and phytosanitary checks at EU borders. Seed potatoes came up. The Spectator used it to roast Labour's Lords shake-up. The Agency is just glad someone in government released something we could all hear clearly.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's theme is the paper trail. The Bigfoot file debunks itself. The UFO file refuses to say anything. The missing chats can't say anything. And the only clear audio of the day is a baroness doing Elton John. A document dump isn't the truth. It's homework. Read the memo, check the stride, and question everybody, especially us.

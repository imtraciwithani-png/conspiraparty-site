Not a Coincidence, It Was a Coincidence

Posted 11/15/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

It's Sunday, and the Agency's corkboard is mostly sticky notes that say "NOT A COINCIDENCE" in all caps. Half the internet decided a Microsoft outage was an attack. The White House's reported UFO point man got promoted to a job with no time for UFOs. Nessie is having her best year since 2019. And somewhere in Wyoming, a Sasquatch has finally been taken. Grab your red string. Try not to tangle it.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The outage everyone solved, wrongly

Microsoft 365 went down late Thursday and stayed wobbly into Friday, taking Outlook, Teams and Copilot with it. Microsoft said there was "no indication this is the result of malicious or cyber activity," and later added, "This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI / agents," per USA Today. By Saturday the company said services were fully restored and blamed an issue introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade, per France 24.

The internet had other ideas. A media analytics firm looked at about 144,000 posts and found the conspiracy share of the conversation tripled from about 6% on day one to 18% on day two. The most popular theory was layoffs (39% of the named theories), followed by a supposed link to an unrelated Nvidia shake-up (23%). Accounts flagged as likely bots wrote about a quarter of the conspiracy posts, per PeakMetrics.

So the theory that "nobody is minding the machines" was pushed hardest by machines that nobody is minding. The Agency would like to award those bots our highest honor, the Golden Irony. Meanwhile the real villain, "a network capacity upgrade," is the most boring supervillain since the guy who invented the reply-all button. Somewhere an IT worker is reading the theories and thinking, "I wish I were that interesting."

Exhibit B: The UFO czar got a bigger desk

Stephen Miller is the new White House chief of staff, per the White House announcement, which mentions border security and crime stats and not one single flying saucer. That matters to the disclosure crowd because Miller had been described as the administration's point man on UAPs. Rep. Eric Burlison, who's leading a House UAP investigation, told Ask a Pol that Trump "took Stephen Miller off the UAP topic," called the new job "basically wartime consigliere," and called it "an unfortunate outcome" for people who care about the issue. UAP Globe notes no official document has ever named who holds the UFO portfolio, before or after.

Let's appreciate the plot. The official UFO job doesn't officially exist, the guy who unofficially had it got promoted, and nobody has announced who unofficially has it now. That isn't a cover-up, it's an org chart drawn in invisible ink. Believers think disclosure got shelved. Skeptics think there was never a shelf. And Washington, both parties, keeps proving it can lose track of a portfolio even faster than a radar blip.

Exhibit C: Nessie's comeback tour

The Official Loch Ness Monster Sightings Register has accepted 15 sightings in 2026, up from six in all of 2025, per Fox News. One sailor reported a "large oversized crocodile head." A visitor described a "very large eel." It's the second-busiest year of the century so far, behind 2019's 18, per the Loch Ness Experience. Statistical ecologist Charles Paxton of the University of St Andrews says there's "nothing special" about the jump, because "the numbers fluctuate a lot year on year." He also noted that after the 1930s sightings, the local government cut its tourism budget because the monster was doing the advertising for free.

That's the greatest marketing story ever told. A mysterious hump in the water did more for the Highlands than any ad agency. Nessie has outlasted every tourism director in Scotland, never asked for a raise, and keeps her social media presence perfectly blurry. Real or not, she's the best-run brand in Europe.

Exhibit D: Wyoming finally caught a Sasquatch, then lost it

In Garrett, Wyoming, a carved wooden Sasquatch that had been peeking out from behind a roadside tree since 2022, complete with a "Please Do Not Feed The Sasquatch" sign, was stolen, per Cowboy State Daily. Neighbors are offering a $100 reward and have, in a first for the field, clear, non-blurry photos of the suspect. Resident Linda Fittje was not amused: "The asshats left the screws in the tree." She also stressed that she only wants sightings of this particular Sasquatch, not any others.

Finally, a Bigfoot case with real evidence: photos, a known address, a sign, and screws left at the scene. If this Sasquatch turns up in someone's living room, it'll be the first confirmed capture in history, and the scientific community will have to admit it was a woodworking project all along.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's theme: "not a coincidence" usually is one. A network upgrade broke email. An org chart moved a guy. Sighting numbers bounced like they always do. And the only Sasquatch with a paper trail was bolted to a tree. The skeptic move is the boring one: read the source, check the numbers, and ask who's posting the theory. Sometimes it's a bot. Then laugh.

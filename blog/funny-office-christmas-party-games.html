Office Christmas parties have a reputation: dry chicken, awkward small talk, and one person who dances too hard. Funny office Christmas party games fix that. The trick is picking activities that feel festive, a little competitive, and safe for a mixed crowd of interns and executives.

Below are game ideas that actually get people laughing — plus printable kits you can download tonight and run at the holiday party, Friendsgiving, or a remote-hybrid meetup.

What makes an office Christmas game work

  • Low props. If it needs a projector and three adapters, skip it.
  • Optional participation. Spectators should still enjoy watching.
  • No humiliation. Embarrassing is fine. Mean is not.
  • Short rounds. 5–10 minutes keeps energy up between snacks.

1. Santa is the suspect (printable mystery / cards)

Lean into a playful “investigation” theme. Our Santa Is Watching kit turns the Christmas party into a light conspiracy: Naughty or Nice cards, elf bingo and a cookie mystery vibe. Guests can play in teams by department. Winner gets the last cookie. Losers get more cookies. Everyone wins.

Pair it with the free Santa Nice List Certificate as a gag award for “Most Likely to Reorganize the Break Room.”

2. Who Leaked the Files? (best for 6–12 people)

If your office party is a smaller team dinner or a breakout room, run Who Leaked the Files? It’s a printable whodunit: someone leaked the files, everyone has a motive, and accusations fly. It feels like a game night, not a forced icebreaker. Print character sheets ahead, stick them in envelopes, and hand them out with name tags.

3. Conspiracy PowerPoint Night (the sleeper hit)

Give everyone a ridiculous holiday theory to pitch in 60 seconds with one slide. Examples: “Why the office microwave is haunted,” “Evidence the holiday party budget was redirected to pigeons,” “The printer knows too much.” Our Conspiracy PowerPoint Night kit includes topic cards, host slides and awards so you don’t have to invent the structure.

Remote teams can share screens. In-person teams can project one deck. Award categories like “Most Confident Wrong Answer” and “Best Use of Clip Art.”

4. White elephant with a twist

Classic white elephant, but every gift must be “suspicious.” Sock gifts, mugs, stickers and fridge kits from our white elephant gift guide section work perfectly. Steal rules stay the same. Add one house rule: when you steal, you must briefly explain the “cover story” for why you need it.

Solid under-desk gifts already on our site: Conspiracy Theory Socks, the Well Informed Mug, and the Bigfoot sticker.

5. Holiday conspiracy bingo

Print bingo cards with squares like “someone mentions Q4,” “cookie disappears,” “playlist skips to Mariah,” “boss tells a story from 2019.” Call squares as the party goes. First blackout wins a stocking stuffer. Our free Holiday Conspiracy Party Pack includes holiday bingo and topic cards if you want a ready-made set.

6. Two truths and a lie — holiday edition

Each person shares two true holiday facts and one lie. Table votes. Works seated. Works sober. Works after one cider. Upgrade: require the lie to sound like a tiny conspiracy (“I once met Santa at a rest stop”).

8. Remote / hybrid friendly options

  • Shared bingo card on screen + chat reactions
  • PowerPoint Night over video with a countdown timer
  • Our free phone game with a room code so people join from couches: conspiraparty-game.onrender.com

Sample 90-minute office party schedule

  1. 0:00 – Arrival, labeled snacks, name tags with fake “clearance levels”
  2. 0:15 – Nice List certificates + quick bingo warm-up
  3. 0:35 – White elephant or Who Leaked the Files
  4. 1:05 – PowerPoint Night lightning round (optional)
  5. 1:25 – Awards, group photo at the foil hat station, done

9. Awards people will actually want

Skip participation trophies. Print funny certificates: “Most Likely to Reply-All,” “Best Cover Story,” “Keeper of the Good Snacks,” “Employee Least Likely to Be a Lizard.” Our free Nice List certificate is an easy template — write the gag title in the name line and hand it over with a candy cane.

If you have a small budget, pair awards with stickers or socks from the stocking stuffer list. People remember the joke longer than the gift card amount.

10. HR-friendly humor boundaries

Keep jokes about printers, pigeons, microwave mysteries and holiday logistics. Avoid anything that targets coworkers’ bodies, beliefs or real-world politics. If a game prompt feels spicy, rewrite it to be about the building (“the fourth-floor thermostat is sentient”) instead of a person. The room stays fun and nobody needs a Monday meeting about Friday.

Host tip: preview every topic card before the party. Toss anything that made you grimace alone in the kitchen.

11. Supplies checklist you can email to the planning committee

  • Printed game sheets / kits in labeled folders
  • Name tags + fake clearance stickers
  • Timer (phone is fine) and a cheap prize bag
  • Speaker for playlist + one backup aux cable
  • Foil for emergency hats, tape, scissors, markers
  • White elephant number slips if you’re doing a swap

Put one person on “print duty” two days early. Party games die when the office printer jams at 4:55.

12. Food stations that double as conversation starters

Set three labeled stations: “Cleared Snacks,” “Under Investigation,” and “Do Not Open Until December 26.” Put perfectly normal food in each. The joke does the networking for shy coworkers. Add a clipboard where people can “sign the chain of custody” when they take a brownie. It sounds ridiculous. It works every year.

Drink tickets can be tiny “temporary clearance” cards. After two, they expire. Hydration never looked so classified.

13. When leadership is in the room

If executives attend, open with bingo or white elephant before PowerPoint Night. Let the loud games happen after people have food in their hands. Invite a senior person to present one intentionally bad theory — it gives everyone permission to be silly. Keep their topic harmless (office plants, parking garage legends, the haunted badge printer).

End on time. The best office parties are the ones that finish before anyone starts checking trains. Send a one-photo recap the next morning with a link to free printables for next year.

14. Reuse kit for next year’s kickoff

Store printed extras in a labeled folder. January all-hands icebreakers, summer picnic “mystery,” and Halloween all recycle the same badges and label style. That’s how a one-time Etsy download becomes a company tradition without another planning committee.

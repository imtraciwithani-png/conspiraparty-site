Field Report

For the Birds

Posted 11/21/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

Saturday's file is all about the boring answer nobody wants. A famous UFO hunter thinks the Pentagon's new videos might be birds. The same files hold a photo of a so-called neck implant with no lab report. The First Lady mounted a defense of the McDonald's diet. Darts fans blamed a video game. And an airline solved the mystery of the brown goo. Grab the red string and a napkin.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The UFO files are for the birds (maybe)

Yesterday the Pentagon dropped another batch of UFO files and told America to make up its own mind. Harvard physicist Avi Loeb, a man who has spent years hoping the sky is more interesting than it looks, made up his. Per NewsNation, he said he was "disappointed" that the release had only three short videos, and that "they might be all birds." He added that the radar data doesn't show it was even the same object each time.

Let's appreciate the twist. The guy most likely to say "alien" looked at the government's footage and said "pigeon." The Birds Aren't Real crowd is now in a tough spot. If the UFOs are birds, and birds are government drones, then the Pentagon just declassified its own surveillance pigeons. Somebody call a congressional hearing. Bring breadcrumbs.

Exhibit B: The neck chip with no lab report

The same file drop includes something far stranger than a pigeon. According to IBTimes UK and NewsNation, a contractor report in the release has a photo and description of a small object said to be removed from a Brazilian military officer's neck. It's about a centimeter wide and was stored in yellow liquid. What the file doesn't include is a lab analysis saying what it actually is.

So here's what's on the record: a photo, a jar and a claim. Here's what's not: a lab report. Believers see an alien tracking chip. Doctors see something they'd want to biopsy before naming. The Agency sees the most expensive mystery ever kept in what looks like a pickle jar. Unanswered question for your next party: if aliens can cross galaxies, why does their tech look like a vitamin?

Exhibit C: The Big Mac Defense

Over in politics, the First Lady went on Fox Business and got a question she clearly didn't want. Per AL.com and the New York Post, Melania Trump said the president's diet is "good," that he has McDonald's sometimes, mostly when traveling, and that she likes it too but doesn't eat it often.

The internet did what the internet does and trolled her. Fair roast on all sides: the White House would like you to believe the most famous fast-food fan in America eats like a marathon runner. His critics treat a cheeseburger like a cover-up worthy of a special counsel. And the TV host who asked about the president's "bad diet" on a business channel has apparently run out of business. The Agency's official position: nobody's diet is as good as their spouse says on TV, including ours.

Exhibit D: Did a video game throw a darts match?

Sports fans have conspiracy theories too, and this one is beautiful. Per talkSPORT and Dartsnews, when teen darts star Luke Littler lost 10-8 to Jermaine Wattimena at the Grand Slam of Darts, some fans decided he wanted to get home to play GTA VI at its midnight launch. Commentator Ian Danter called that "far too simplistic" and gave credit to Wattimena, who averaged over 100.

This is the purest conspiracy theory of the week. It has a motive (a video game), timing (midnight), and a suspect with a controller. It just skips the boring part where the other guy played really well. That's the whole conspiracy starter kit in one darts match.

Exhibit E: The brown goo files

Finally, a mystery that actually got solved. Dozens of bags came off an American Airlines flight in Atlanta covered in brown sludge, and passengers assumed the worst. Per UPI and WSB-TV, the airline says it was peanut butter that leaked out of one passenger's checked bag. Somebody packed enough peanut butter to frost a cargo hold. That's not a conspiracy. That's a sandwich with ambition.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's theme is the boring answer. The UFO might be a bird. The chip needs a lab. The diet involves fries. The darts loss involved a better opponent. The goo was peanut butter. The boring answer isn't always right, but it deserves a seat at the table before the aliens get one. Ask the dumb questions, check the sources, and question everybody, especially us.

Pardon the Interruption: The Ballroom Birds

Posted 11/26/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

Happy Thanksgiving, agents. Today’s file is about things that were supposed to be hidden and absolutely were not. Two turkeys got a pardon and a real estate pitch. A monk says he smuggled NASA UFO footage onto a PlayStation game. The internet scheduled the end of civilization and the end of civilization didn’t show up. And a government discovered the deep state has a passport. Loosen your belt and grab the red string.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The ballroom birds

Per Axios and the White House, President Trump pardoned two West Virginia turkeys, Freedom and Feathers, on the South Lawn Tuesday. Both are retiring to Texas A&M. Before the pardon, he told the crowd the White House had “lacked appropriate accommodations to host such distinguished guests” for 200 years, and announced that next year the birds can visit “that rather small ballroom being built.” On their names: “I’ve heard better.” According to a White House fact sheet cited by Axios, Freedom’s favorite treat is Dr Pepper.

Let’s review the evidence. Two four-month-old birds were driven to Washington, put up at a fancy hotel, told their names were mid, and handed a ballroom brochure. That’s not a pardon, that’s a timeshare presentation. Freedom survived the oven and the sales pitch, which is more than most of us manage at a family reunion. Fair to everyone: every president turns this event into a stand-up set. Past administrations used turkey puns. This one used a floor plan.

Exhibit B: The monk’s PlayStation disc

Cybernews and dev.ua report that Josen Takano, a monk who runs a UFO museum in Hakui, Japan, told the American Alchemy podcast he copied NASA UFO photos and videos during visits to Johnson Space Center in the early 1990s. He says the footage ended up as bonus material in a 1996 PlayStation game, Junichi Yaoi: Top Secret Project: Chase the UFO. Did NASA know? “Oh, I didn’t tell them.” A Redditor then used Linux and some AI-written Python code to pull 90 videos off the disc. Nobody has confirmed any of it is actually NASA footage, and some users say the clips were already out there.

We have so many questions, and the first one is: you had to beat the game? The most important secret in human history was gated behind a final boss. Disclosure via cheat code. The footage reportedly looks like “fireflies floating around,” which is the official description of every UFO video ever taken. Still, credit to the Redditor. Congress has held hearings for years and gotten less footage than one guy with a 1996 disc and a free weekend.

Exhibit C: The blackout that wasn’t

Stateline reports that a string of unrelated outages this month (Microsoft, an FAA ground stop blamed on a router upgrade in Arkansas, several banks, Apple) plus a scheduled nationwide Emergency Alert System test convinced a chunk of the internet that a coordinated collapse was coming. TikTok warnings about a 90-day blackout starting Nov. 17 spread fast. Snopes called it unfounded. FEMA’s response on Facebook: “We love a good fan theory as much as anyone, but there is no secret emergency happening here.”

Nov. 17 came and went, and the lights are on, the pies are in the oven and somebody’s uncle has 400 cans of beans in the garage. To be fair to the preppers, a router in Arkansas grounding planes nationwide really does sound like a conspiracy. It’s just a dumber one. The real threat to America’s grid is one contractor and a loose cable. Also, a federal agency saying “fan theory” is the most chill debunk in history. Write that intern a raise.

Exhibit D: The deep state has a passport

OC Media and Civil Georgia report that after talks with Russia didn’t get Georgian wine back on Russian shelves, and Moscow floated sanctioning more wine companies, Georgia’s prime minister Irakli Kobakhidze suggested the “deep state” might be behind Moscow’s position. His ruling party usually blames the deep state for trouble coming from the West.

Skeptic’s note: once the deep state is blamed for both sides of a fight, it stops being a theory and starts being a weather report. Every politician on Earth has learned the same move. Bad news? Shadowy forces. Good news? Personal genius. We’re impressed the deep state has time to run Washington, Brussels and Moscow and still hold up a wine shipment.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today, the cover-ups covered nothing. The turkeys were pardoned in public with a ballroom ad. The “stolen NASA footage” was sitting on a game disc for 30 years. The secret blackout was announced on TikTok weeks early. And the deep state is now so busy it needs a travel agent. The truth isn’t out there. It’s in the bonus features. Happy Thanksgiving. Check your sources before you check the pie.

Old Broken Stuff, Toilets and the Illuminati

Posted 11/14/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

It's Saturday, and the Agency's corkboard looks like a museum gift shop exploded on it. The whole country got grounded over "old broken stuff." A presidential library put its weirdest presents on display. The Illuminati quietly turned 250. And if you saw a UFO from your stranded airport gate, the Pentagon would like you to know it is not taking your call. Grab your red string and a neck pillow.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The official cause is "old broken stuff"

On Friday afternoon the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop that lasted more than four hours, the longest in 25 years. Officials said new technology being added to the air traffic control system overloaded antiquated parts of it and caused gaps in radar and communications, per AP. Reuters counted nearly 10,000 canceled flights by late Friday, per Reuters. The president's explanation, as relayed on Fox News: a "bunch of old broken stuff," some of it 55 years old, that Congress was asked to replace and hasn't, per The Daily Beast. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said it wasn't a cyberattack and added, "This is what the leadership of President Trump looks like."

The Agency has questions, mainly for everyone. To the White House: you can't call a five-hour national shutdown a leadership showcase. To Congress, both parties: you've been told about the 55-year-old equipment for years, and somehow the money always went to a commemorative coin. And to the internet, which blamed hackers and aliens before the first plane moved: sometimes the truth really is a cable from 1971 held on with tape.

Exhibit B: The presidential re-gifting closet

The Clinton Presidential Center in Little Rock is running "The Big. The Bold. The Bizarre," more than 90 odd gifts and keepsakes from six presidential libraries, through February 14, per the Clinton Foundation. Highlights from the Democrat-Gazette's tour: two teeth from Harry Truman's dental bridge, a saxophone-shaped chair, a toilet piggy bank with Rush Limbaugh's face in the bowl, a toilet seat with Saddam Hussein on the lid, and a J. Edgar Hoover ashtray etched with JFK's government ID and ringed with his actual fingerprints.

About that ashtray. The FBI director gave the president a gift with the president's own fingerprints on it, to sit on his desk, every day. That's not a present. That's a hostage note with a place for cigars. Meanwhile, the bipartisan toilet section proves one thing: no matter who's in office, somebody out there is crafting a commode about it. That's democracy, folks.

Exhibit C: Happy 250th, Illuminati

The Spectator is reviewing a new history of the Illuminati, the Bavarian secret society a law professor named Adam Weishaupt started in 1776. It was suppressed in the 1780s, and Weishaupt ended up a "cranky exile" denying it had ever existed, per The Spectator. The review cites polls finding more than 40 percent of Americans think an "Illuminati-style" group secretly runs the world. It started with Weishaupt and five of his students, per HISTORY.

So the group that supposedly runs the planet lasted about a decade, began as a professor and five students, and was shut down by Bavaria's ruler. The most feared shadow cabal in history has the same origin story as a fantasy football league. America and the Illuminati are the same age this year. Only one of them can keep its planes in the air, and it's not clear which.

Exhibit D: Please hold, your UFO is important to us

The Pentagon's UFO office, AARO, still has no way for ordinary civilians to report a sighting. Its online form is only for current or former government workers, troops and contractors who know about government UAP programs. Civilian pilots are told to report to air traffic control, and the FAA passes those reports along, per AARO's own page, which says it "will announce when a reporting mechanism is available to the public."

Read that again on a Friday when air traffic control went down. The only public pipeline for UFO reports runs through the exact system that was being held together with old broken stuff. The Agency is not saying that's a cover-up. We're saying it's the most government thing that has ever happened, and we've seen the ashtray.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's theme: the truth is usually old, dusty and embarrassing. Aging radar grounded a nation. A library's weirdest treasure is a fingerprint flex. The world's most feared secret society was a ten-year college club. And the UFO hotline is "please call back later." The skeptic move is the boring one: read the source, check the date, and blame the duct tape before the lizards. Then laugh.

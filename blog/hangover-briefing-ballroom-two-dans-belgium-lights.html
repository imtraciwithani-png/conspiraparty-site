Hangover Briefing

Posted 11/05/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

The midterms are over, the confetti is sticky and America has entered the traditional 48-hour period where everyone invents a new math problem. While the networks argue over who gets to be dramatic, the Agency filed today's hangover: a ballroom with a missile-proof ceiling, an Alaska race with too many Dans, a British pilot who cannot catch the lights and a Capitol suddenly rediscovering that UFOs exist. Coffee optional. Open the folder.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: The most beautiful crisis response in the world

According to a roundup circulating via Yahoo / BuzzFeed, the day after Republicans took a thrashing, President Trump gathered reporters at the site of a new presidential ballroom. He called it "the most beautiful in the world," bragged that the roof could hold "as many helicopters as you want," and described the ceiling as "drone-proof, missile-proof, bullet-proof." There were also chandeliers "all over," a 50-foot ceiling with a "beautiful glass tube," a top-of-the-line kitchen and a steel beam he signed "Love!" Then, per the same report, he posted about Daylight Saving Time and the stock market — and managed not to mention the midterm losses at all.

Our analysts salute the craft. When the map goes blue, the correct response is apparently architecture. If your party just got rejected by half the country, invent a room that can stop a missile and put helicopters on it. Somewhere a crisis manager is weeping into a PowerPoint that begins with "Message discipline."

Exhibit B: The two Dans problem

Meanwhile in Alaska, the Senate race between Republican Dan S. Sullivan and Democrat Mary Peltola was still tight. Enter independent candidate Dan J. Sullivan Jr. The New Republic reports that on Thursday the president posted that vote totals for the "Fake Dan Sullivan," plus another candidate he called a "slug," "should be immediately given to the REAL Dan Sullivan," and that "there is no other fair solution!" Combining the two Sullivans would, on paper, nudge the Republican ahead — and, as the same report notes, would also violate the law.

What's on the record: Alaska uses ranked-choice voting, results were still coming in and an Anchorage voter told CNN (quoted in that report) that you can "absolutely" tell who's who on the ballot even without glasses. What's on the corkboard: when democracy produces two people with the same first and last name, some people reach for a calculator instead of a recount. Our Agency rule remains: if your election theory requires merging two humans into one, it needs more coffee and less math.

Exhibit C: Seven flights and zero catch-ups

Palate cleanser from the sky. Metro reports that Steve Frazer, a pilot of 27 years from Cheshire, says he has seen the same flashing lights six or seven times since 2022, including over Brussels and routes involving the Netherlands and Lithuania. He initially figured military refueling. Other pilots radioed about military activity. He filmed them, compared them to Starlink and says he never quite caught up. UFOlogists told Metro the footage deserves logging; one also floated secret drones as the boring explanation, which is the skeptic's favorite kind of boring.

Nearly one in three Brits, per the YouGov numbers Metro cites, already believe aliens have visited. The UK closed its Ministry of Defence UFO hotline years ago. So the official process is: see weird lights, film weird lights, talk to Metro, argue on the internet. Classic Agency workflow.

Exhibit D: Disclosure cosplay, now with a majority

Before the other team gets smug: Cybernews reports Democrats have drafted plans for a House UAP committee if they regain Congress, with Rep. Andre Carson arguing disclosure has been too slow. Whistleblower David Grusch, quoted there from a Rogan appearance, has said he does not trust "another president" on the topic. So the pattern is familiar. When one party holds the gavel, the other discovers aliens are urgent. When the gavel flips, so does the urgency. The lights over Belgium did not check party registration.

Exhibit E: Odd-news side quests

From today's Weird News feed: NASA teased an experimental X-70 "Fractal" aircraft (The Debrief orbit), Russia is reportedly treating pizza as Western soft power that should leave schools and someone is still asking whether a Fort Worth Democrat can take office after winning a seat she had dropped out of. None of these involve merging two Dans. Progress.

The pattern on the corkboard

Connect the dots and today's theme is substitution. Substitute a ballroom for a postmortem. Substitute vote algebra for vote counting. Substitute a House committee for actual answers about lights in the sky. The skeptic move stays the same: dig in yourself, read what's on the record and leave room for unanswered questions. Chandeliers are beautiful. They are not a briefing.

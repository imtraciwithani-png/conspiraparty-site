The Morning After

Posted 11/04/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

The votes are in, the coffee is cold and the internet has already filed 40,000 theories about what happened. While the pundits argue over who gets to be smug, the Agency sorted the morning-after files: a county full of orange bags, a man who was accused of vanishing while standing right there, a hat with one word on it, a party that found the good wine and a space probe having a worse week than any campaign manager. Open the folder.

Exhibit A: The great orange bag mystery

On Tuesday morning, ballot scanners across Westchester County, New York stopped accepting paper ballots. AP reports officials blamed a programming glitch from a software update for one municipal contest that was made after the machines were prepared, and a judge let them move thousands of ballots to a secure warehouse for counting. NBC News says voters dropped their ballots into secure orange emergency bags under bipartisan control. WIRED notes the county first blamed "the thickness of the paper ballots," then quietly edited that line out 20 minutes later, and that researchers clocked conspiratorial posts about it at roughly 150 a minute.

Within minutes, some big accounts declared Democrats were cheating. The Republican whose seat was actually on the line, Rep. Mike Lawler, told Fox News, per PBS: "There's no shenanigans." Our analysts note that when the guy who'd benefit most from yelling "rigged" declines to yell it, the theory has a staffing problem. Also, any plot that hinges on paper thickness was clearly planned by someone who has never met a printer.

Exhibit B: The commissioner who went missing while being present

Then came a rumor inside the rumor. A reporter posted that Westchester's Republican elections commissioner, Doug Colety, was a "no-show." According to Raw Story's report, Colety said "I've been here since seven in the morning. I'll be here until five in the morning," and Lawler posted that Colety had called his campaign at 6:02 a.m. to warn them. The headline quote says it all: "No conspiracy theory here." Respect to a man who had to prove he existed on the busiest day of his year.

Exhibit C: The hat that says VETO

With Democrats projected to take both chambers, President Trump posted, per Axios: "I wasn't on the Ballot and if I was, we would have won IN A LANDSLIDE!" This after weeks of telling rallies to pretend he was on the ballot. The Guardian reports he blamed Fox News, of all networks, and USA Today says he posted a mockup of his red hat reading "VETO" while also saying he's willing to accept the results and work with Democrats. So the official position is Schrödinger's ballot: he was on it when it was going well and off it by midnight.

Exhibit D: The wine began to flow freely

Before anyone on the other side gets too comfortable: The Hill reports House Democrats partied at a trendy hotel bar where Hakeem Jeffries declared "the great American comeback," and the reporter literally wrote "the wine began to flow freely." DNC chair Ken Martin told NPR the results were the party's best in a century and that candidates "ran for something, not against something." Bold claim from a party whose top campaign issue was, broadly, a different guy. Every winner calls it a mandate. Every loser calls it the candidates. Both are reading the same tea leaves upside down.

Exhibit E: Mystery texts in Georgia

A quick one with no punchline yet. PBS reports some Fulton County voters got texts about polling place changes that the county says it didn't send, and watchdogs say it's unclear who sent them. That's a genuine unanswered question, which is rarer than you'd think on a day like this. File it under "watch this space."

Exhibit F: Even Voyager had a glitchy Tuesday

Palate cleanser. The Debrief reports NASA's Voyager 1 is having "unexpected" problems engineers have never seen before, nearly 50 years into the trip. Meanwhile Coast to Coast AM is circulating a game camera clip that might be Bigfoot, or might be a very committed hiker. We checked, and neither one voted in Westchester.

The pattern on the corkboard

Connect the dots and today's theme is glitches. A scanner glitch, a rumor glitch, a ballot that someone was on and then wasn't, a mandate that everyone discovers after winning, and a probe in interstellar space throwing error codes. The skeptic move: when something breaks, ask what's on the record before you ask who's to blame. Usually the answer is a software update, and sometimes it's a mystery text. Either way, judge it yourself.

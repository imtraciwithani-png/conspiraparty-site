Beads, Cakes and a Soviet Space Rock

Posted 11/12/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

It's Thursday, and the Agency's corkboard is covered in people jumping the gun. The White House says investigating it might be a crime. A congresswoman says the cake isn't done. A prime minister's son says he's reporting from inside the Matrix. And a space rock might be a Soviet probe in a very good disguise. Grab your red string.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: Subpoenas like Mardi Gras beads

White House counsel Will Scharf sent a letter to Rep. Robert Garcia, the Democrat set to chair the House Oversight Committee in January, warning that some of the probes Democrats are promising "may cross the line into blatant criminality," per CBS News. The letter quoted Democrats bragging they'd hand out subpoenas "like Mardi Gras beads" and "like candy," reports NBC News. Garcia's answer: "It appears Donald Trump has something to hide," and he told NPR they won't be "intimidated." A Cato Institute scholar told CBS the letter is more political than legal and that a criminal case over a subpoena vote is "incredibly unlikely."

So the Agency has questions for both sides of the aisle. To the White House: announcing that being investigated might be a crime is the single most suspicious sentence a government can say out loud. That's the "nothing to see here" sign, but on letterhead. To the Democrats: you haven't even been sworn in and you're already promising subpoenas like party favors? Oversight is supposed to be a scalpel, not a parade float. Somewhere a legal intern is laminating subpoenas in festive colors. Everyone calm down. It's November. Mardi Gras is in February.

Exhibit B: The cake is still in the oven

Rep. Lauren Boebert hasn't conceded Colorado's 4th District after CNN projected Democrat Eileen Laubacher the winner. "Declaring victory now is like icing a cake before it comes out of the oven," Boebert said, and counties have until November 13 to finish curing and counting ballots, per The Colorado Sun. As of Wednesday evening Laubacher led 48.79% to 47.91% and the AP hadn't called it, reports KKTV. Boebert also said she won't run again in 2028 if the final count shows she lost. Meanwhile X stuck a community note under her video, which HuffPost called the most embarrassing detail of all.

Look, "wait until every legal ballot is counted" is technically the correct civics answer, and the Agency will not be mad at anyone for respecting a deadline. But the baking metaphor is doing a lot of work. The cake has been in the oven for nine days. The smoke alarm is going off. The neighbors called. And Laubacher, for her part, declared victory before the AP did, which is the political version of eating the frosting straight from the tub. Friday is the official taste test.

Exhibit C: A report from inside the Matrix (available for preorder)

Yair Netanyahu, son of Israel's prime minister, has a book coming out called Two-Front War. The publisher's blurb says that when Israel's "prosecutors, judges, intelligence bureaucracy, military command, media networks, and foreign-funded NGOs" turned toward removing his father from power, he found himself in "a Kafkaesque system," and it calls the book his "report from inside the Matrix," per the Simon & Schuster listing. It's published by RatPac Press, 288 pages, $32.99. Middle East Eye notes he's repeated claims on X that the recent election was "stolen."

The deep state, it turns out, is a global franchise now, like Starbucks, and every country gets its own seasonal menu. The Agency has no position on Israeli politics beyond this: when your theory of the plot against your dad includes basically every institution in the country, that's not a conspiracy, that's a group project. Also, a word of advice from the people who sell red string for a living. If you escaped the Matrix, you probably don't need a hardcover deal with a list price. Morpheus never charged $32.99.

Exhibit D: The asteroid that might be a Soviet probe

Here's one for the corkboard. A roughly 36-foot object called 1998 KY26 is speeding up in a way gravity alone doesn't explain, and Harvard astrophysicist Avi Loeb and co-authors argue it could be Phobos 1, a Soviet Mars probe lost in 1988, per Fox News. Their paper says it spins once every 5.35 minutes and is unusually shiny, and admits "an investigation of this kind cannot be conclusive." Meanwhile a separate Georgia Tech study says it's likely too warm to be a "dark comet." NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said, "nothing is about to hit us," and he's seen no evidence it's anything other than an asteroid. Japan's Hayabusa2 is scheduled to visit it in 2031.

The tabloid headline said it's "hurtling toward Earth." The actual distance is around 189 million miles, per The Sun, which is a very relaxed hurtle. But the Agency respects the plot: a Soviet spacecraft goes silent in 1988 after a bad software command, then spends 38 years pretending to be a rock. That's the most committed undercover operation in history. If it's true, it deserves a medal and a very confused apology from every astronomer who called it a comet.

The pattern on the corkboard

Today's theme: everybody wants to call it early. The White House wants to call investigations crimes before they start. One candidate wants to call victory before the AP does, and the other wants to keep the oven on. A memoir calls the whole country a conspiracy. And the internet called a space rock a Soviet ghost ship before the spacecraft that could check it even arrives. The skeptic move is the boring one: wait for the count, read the paper, check the distance. Then laugh.

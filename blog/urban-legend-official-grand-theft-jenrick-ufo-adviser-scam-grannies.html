This Is Not an Urban Legend

Posted 11/19/2026 · ← All field reports

Full disclosure: this is commentary and jokes. Facts come from the linked reports, so click through and judge for yourself. The party kits linked here are our own products.

Thursday's theme arrived pre-printed on a government website: "This is not an urban legend." Today's files are about the moment the fringe gets a lanyard and an official title. A conspiracy YouTuber gets a Cabinet Office gig. A politician casts himself as a video game hero. A UFO whistleblower gets a desk on Capitol Hill. And a gang of AI grannies gets hired to waste scammers' time. Red string at the ready.

XFacebookPinterest

Exhibit A: Tokyo hires the urban legend guy

Japan's government launched a security-awareness feature on its official PR site this week, aimed at people in their 20s and 30s, starring a YouTuber called Naokiman. His main channel has about 2.53 million subscribers and covers conspiracies and urban legends. The page's tagline is literally "This is not an urban legend," per The Asahi Shimbun. The Japan Times reports he has previously spread unsubstantiated claims, including that a major disaster would strike in July 2025. In his own video he said, "What comes out of my mouth is no longer an urban legend; it's become official."

Opposition leader Yuichiro Tamaki said it "gives official backing to information that is not based on facts." Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara said a contractor proposed him through competitive bidding, and that the government didn't comb through all his past posts, per The Mainichi. The youth PR budget is capped at ¥150 million, roughly $940,000.

Fair roast for everybody: the government said "we didn't check," which is the official motto of every conspiracy theory ever written. And the YouTuber now has the ultimate merch line: "Approved by the Cabinet Office." The Agency respects the hustle. But if your plan to fight misinformation is hiring the misinformation's favorite storyteller, the drama they're shooting, about a housewife whose mystery app makes food and energy vanish, is the second-weirdest thing in the budget.

Exhibit B: Grand Theft Jenrick

Hours before GTA 6 dropped, Reform UK's Treasury spokesman Robert Jenrick posted an AI-made parody trailer called "Grand Theft Jenrick," with himself as the hero, per The Standard. His avatar leaps between yachts, is told his "next job" is to "cut the cost of living," and informs a shoplifter, "That's not what self-checkout means." In a follow-up clip, Nigel Farage "plays" the game with a controller that one critic noted wasn't even turned on, per The Independent.

Metro noted the AI sometimes forgets what he looks like, which is the most relatable thing a computer has ever done. The Agency's take: a law-and-order politician starring in a game famous for, well, the opposite, is a bold branding choice. His opponents are piling on, and his fans are asking for a sequel. Our only theory is that every politician's campaign video is now one prompt away from a boss fight. Lock up your controllers.

Exhibit C: The UFO whistleblower gets a staff badge

Rep. Eric Burlison (R-MO) announced that Air Force veteran Jake Barber has joined his office as a senior adviser on UAP, per Burlison's press release. Barber went public in January 2025 with accounts of UAP-recovery operations. He'll help with investigations, witness interviews and verifying evidence. His own quote: "We're interested in evidence that can stand on its own."

Here's the skeptic's footnote, and Redacted Weekly spells it out: the hire is confirmed, the recovery claims are not independently verified, and the UAP Disclosure Act passed the House inside a defense bill but isn't law yet. So, a job offer is not proof. Still, we love the twist. The man hired for his testimony says testimony isn't enough. That's the most skeptical sentence to come out of Congress all month. If he really does demand measurements and documents, the believers and the debunkers might both have to show their homework. Terrifying.

Exhibit D: Release the grannies

And for dessert: TechRadar reports on a "bizarre AI retirement home" that lets you set a whole neighborhood of AI grannies loose on scam callers. Finally, a government-grade deterrence program nobody had to bid on. If the scammers say they're from the IRS, Grandma Ethel will need to tell them about her hip first. All 40 minutes of it.

The pattern on the corkboard

Every file today is the fringe getting promoted. The urban legend guy gets official. The video game gets a politician. The UFO story gets a staff title. The grannies get an algorithm. A badge doesn't make a claim true, and a laugh doesn't make it false. The Agency's Thursday rule: check who's paying, then check the source, then hand out the red string.

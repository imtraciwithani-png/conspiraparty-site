WEEKLY DIG #01

Gulf of Tonkin — when the SIGINT did not match the sales pitch.

Weekly Dig · Document Vault · Skeptic Tools · Rabbit Hole Map

Sample issue · Oct 30, 2026 · ~6 min dig

The brief

August 1964: a real clash on the 2nd, a disputed "second attack" on the 4th, and a Congressional resolution that opened the door to a wider war. Years later, NSA historical work said the Aug 4 SIGINT did not support the public claim. This is a classic "sounds fake → actually on the record" trail — and a reminder to separate what was sold from what the files show.

Dig in yourself

  • Independent: National Security Archive — Tonkin press note / briefing book
  • Independent: NSAEBB 132 — Gulf of Tonkin collection
  • Mainstream: Wikipedia overview (follow its citations)
  • Primary document: Pentagon Papers at NARA (wider Vietnam decision trail)

What's on the record

NSA released historical materials in 2005–06. Historian Robert J. Hanyok's study argued the Aug 4 attack reporting was skewed. The Gulf of Tonkin Resolution remains a legislative fact; the intelligence picture under it is what shifted.

Still open / talk about it

  1. If SIGINT was clear by 2001 in draft form, why did the public wait years for the release?
  2. How should citizens treat "urgent" war claims the next time timelines move faster than FOIA?
  3. What other modern flashpoints deserve the same claim-vs-release timeline treatment?

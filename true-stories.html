Project MKUltra

CIA · ~1953–1964 (investigated 1975–77)

A CIA “umbrella” program funding behavioral-modification research, including drugs. Many files were destroyed in 1973; surviving records and Church Committee / Senate hearings documented the program. Unwitting testing on U.S. persons was later discontinued after an Inspector General review.

Sources: Senate Intelligence (MKUltra hearing PDF) · Wikipedia overview

Operation Northwoods

Joint Chiefs of Staff · 1962 (declassified later)

A JCS memorandum titled “Justification for US Military Intervention in Cuba” proposed staged pretexts (including fake attacks) to justify invading Cuba. It was not carried out. The document is in the National Archives JFK Collection (record 202-10002-10104) and published by the National Security Archive.

Sources: National Archives catalog · National Security Archive page · NARA PDF

Gulf of Tonkin (Aug 1964)

Vietnam War · NSA historian review declassified 2005

After a real clash on Aug 2, the Johnson administration cited a second Aug 4 attack to push the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution. NSA historian Robert J. Hanyok’s later study concluded the SIGINT showed no second attack and that reporting was skewed. NSA released related materials in 2005–2006.

Sources: NSA Gulf of Tonkin releases · Hanyok Cryptologic Quarterly PDF · National Security Archive press note

USPHS Untreated Syphilis Study at Tuskegee

1932–1972 · CDC / National Archives

A U.S. Public Health Service study followed hundreds of Black men with syphilis without informed consent and without offering treatment even after penicillin became standard. The study ended in 1972 after press exposure; President Clinton apologized in 1997.

Sources: CDC official page · Wikipedia overview

COINTELPRO

FBI · 1956–1971 · Church Committee

FBI “counterintelligence programs” used covert tactics to disrupt domestic groups and individuals. The Church Committee documented the programs from Bureau files. Targets included civil-rights and anti-war organizations among others.

Sources: U.S. Senate: Church Committee overview · FBI Vault: COINTELPRO · Wikipedia overview

CIA & the press (Project MOCKINGBIRD)

Documented wiretap · Mar–Jun 1963 · “Family Jewels”

Popular histories often use “Operation Mockingbird” for alleged broad CIA media control. What is clearly documented in CIA “Family Jewels” material is Project MOCKINGBIRD: a telephone intercept on two Washington newsmen (Mar 12–Jun 15, 1963), authorized at high levels. Broader CIA–journalist relationships were also examined by the Church Committee—keep the popular nickname separate from the specific cryptonym.

Sources: Wikipedia: Operation Mockingbird (context & debates) · Church Committee (Senate overview)

Project Sunshine

AEC / RAND · 1950s fallout research

A U.S. Atomic Energy Commission research effort (with RAND) studied worldwide effects of nuclear weapons, including strontium-90 uptake. The 1953 RAND/AEC report “Worldwide Effects of Atomic Weapons: Project SUNSHINE” is public. Later reporting and scholarship discussed controversial tissue-collection practices used in fallout studies—treat those details carefully and stick to primary docs.

Sources: RAND report PDF (R-251) · Wikipedia overview

Operation Paperclip

U.S. postwar · National Archives

A U.S. program that brought German scientists and engineers (including some with Nazi affiliations) to America after WWII for military and aerospace work. Extensively documented in National Archives records and later histories.

Sources: National Archives topical overview · Wikipedia overview

Political scandals that actually happened

Still documented. Still not a fanfic about your uncle's group chat.

Watergate

1972–1974 · Nixon resignation

What's documented: A break-in at Democratic National Committee offices in the Watergate complex, cover-up, Washington Post reporting, Senate investigation, and President Nixon's resignation. The word "gate" has been stuck to every scandal since — bipartisan branding.

Sources: National Archives education page · Wikipedia overview

Iran–Contra

1980s · Reagan administration investigations

What's documented: Senior officials facilitated arms sales to Iran and diverted funds to Contra rebels in Nicaragua despite congressional restrictions. Independent counsel and congressional hearings followed. A masterclass in "off-books" becoming on-front-page.

Sources: National Archives research hub · Wikipedia overview

Snowden / NSA PRISM disclosures

2013 · Guardian / Washington Post reporting

What's documented: Edward Snowden leaked classified material describing bulk collection programs including PRISM. Major outlets authenticated documents; tech firms disputed "direct access" characterizations; public debate and some reforms followed. Surveillance state: not just a costume.

Sources: Guardian PRISM report · Snowden timeline · Wikipedia: PRISM

Epstein criminal case (court record)

2008 Florida deal · 2019 SDNY indictment · death in custody

What's documented: Jeffrey Epstein faced sex-crime cases; a controversial Florida non-prosecution agreement; a 2019 federal indictment in New York; death in federal jail awaiting trial; civil litigation and later document unsealing. Believers say a single "client list" convicts a cast of famous people. We do not: treat every named associate as proven guilty of Epstein's crimes. Read filings, not just memes.

Sources: SDNY indictment press release · Wikipedia (timeline & citations)

JFK assassination records

1963 · Warren Commission · HSCA · JFK Records Act releases

What's documented: President Kennedy was assassinated in Dallas; Lee Harvey Oswald was arrested and killed; official inquiries disagreed in nuance; huge archives continue to be released. Believers say everything from CIA plots to umbrellas of destiny. Corkboards welcome; invented smoking guns are not.

Sources: National Archives JFK Collection · Wikipedia overview

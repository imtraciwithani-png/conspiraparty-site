Roswell & Area 51

The Claim
A flying saucer crashed near Roswell, New Mexico in 1947, and the government has been hiding alien bodies (and their tech) at Area 51 ever since.
The Real Story
In 1947 the Army first announced a “flying disc,” then said it was a weather balloon. Decades later the Air Force explained the debris came from Project Mogul, a classified balloon program for detecting Soviet nuclear tests. Area 51 is real: a secret test site where spy planes like the U-2 and A-12 were flown, which explains a lot of the strange lights people saw. The government finally acknowledged the base in 2013.

Bigfoot

The Claim
A giant, hairy, ape-like creature roams the forests of North America, leaving huge footprints and dodging every camera ever invented.
The Real Story
There are thousands of sighting reports and plaster footprint casts, but no body, bones or confirmed DNA. The famous 1967 Patterson–Gimlin film is still debated. Scientists point to bears standing upright, misidentifications and a few well-known hoaxes. Still, Bigfoot remains the undefeated hide-and-seek champion of the world.

Birds Aren’t Real

The Claim
Every bird is actually a government surveillance drone. That’s why they sit on power lines: they’re recharging.
The Real Story
“Birds Aren’t Real” is a deliberate parody movement started in 2017 to poke fun at how conspiracy theories spread. Its founder later confirmed it was satire. Birds are, in fact, real. Probably. (Our pigeons have declined to comment.)

CASE 002

Loch Ness

A long lake in the Scottish Highlands, and a maybe-monster who has missed every scheduled photo call since the 1930s.

Suspicion meter

44% — The lake is real. The portrait is a toy.

Evidence

  • Loch Ness is a real freshwater lake, about 23 miles long and very deep.
  • The modern rush of sightings followed a 1933 report in the Inverness Courier.
  • The famous 1934 “surgeon’s photograph” was later described by Christian Spurling, in a 1994 confession reported by the press, as a toy submarine with a head stuck on.

The public record, lightly

  • The Great Glen, which holds the loch, is a real geological fault.
  • Sonar surveys of the loch have been published for decades. None has produced an agreed specimen.

How to use this at your party

Float a green balloon in the punch bowl. Guests file a sighting on a napkin.

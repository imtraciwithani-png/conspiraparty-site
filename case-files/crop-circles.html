CASE 007

Crop circles

Flattened wheat that looks designed. The best-documented designers brought planks and a sense of humor.

Suspicion meter

48% — Art department: two retirees and a board.

Evidence

  • Doug Bower and Dave Chorley told the press in 1991 that they had been making circles in England since the late 1970s with rope and a wooden plank.
  • The patterns are real flattened crops. The overnight part is the impressive bit.
  • Later circles got more elaborate. A plank does not stop being a plank when the drawing gets fancy.

The public record, lightly

  • Wiltshire, England, is the region most associated with the patterns.
  • The 1991 account was a public press story, not a secret memo.

How to use this at your party

Give guests string and paper plates. Best circle wins a badge, not a farm.

How to Bring Up Conspiracy Theories at the Thanksgiving Table

Posted October 2026 · ← All field reports

Every family has a Chaos Uncle (or Chaos Aunt). This is the classified playbook: how to escalate from gravy jokes to political rabbit holes without starting a real war — and how to exit when the pie hits the fan.

Thanksgiving already has tension, carbs and people who “just want to ask a question.” Conspiraparty is here for the entertainment version: silly theories, QAnon-adjacent jokes and real politicians name-dropped like board-game tokens — roasted evenly, left and right. If someone treats a joke as a briefing, you failed the mission. Pass the rolls.

The rules of engagement

  1. Roast all sides or roast none. If you only dunk on one team, you’re campaigning, not hosting a party.
  2. Frame it as a theory / joke. “Some people believe…” and “what if…” beat “here’s the truth.”
  3. No slurs, no hate, no calls to harm. Chaos is funny. Cruelty is not the brand.
  4. Have an exit line ready. The Committee insists.

Escalation levels: Mild Gravy → Full Tinfoil

Level 1 — Mild Gravy

Safe for the kids’ table. Food and household absurdities.

  • “This turkey was switched at the last minute. Who authorized the swap?”
  • “Real cranberry sauce is classified. The can is the cover story.”
  • “Green bean casserole is a Midwest psyop.”
  • “Pie crust was invented by Big Butter.”

Level 2 — Medium Stuffing

Classic harmless conspiracies with a Thanksgiving twist.

  • Point at a bird outside: “Charging station or spy drone?”
  • “Does Aunt Carol’s basement connect to the Denver Airport tunnels?”
  • “The moon landing was filmed in Uncle Rick’s garage. Outtakes?”
  • “Bigfoot is avoiding this family specifically. Vote on why.”

Level 3 — Spicy Cranberry

Political rabbit holes. Still satire. Still both sides.

  • “Which politician is secretly a lizard? You must name one from each party.”
  • “What if the deep state is just group chats that got out of hand?”
  • “Every administration says the last one was the conspiracy. Coincidence? Poll the table.”
  • “The only bipartisan agreement: the other side is in a cult. Gently request receipts.”
  • “What if Q was just a very committed fanfic author?” Keep it a joke. Watch the room.

Level 4 — Full Tinfoil

Dessert-only. High risk. Maximum meme energy.

  • “Pass the gravy and the classified documents.”
  • “Someone checked a ‘drop’ before carving. Guess who — no names.”
  • “Was the midterms ‘stolen’? Cool — now list both sides’ stolen claims.”
  • “Storms aren’t weather — they’re narrative weather. Salt, please.”
  • Toast to whatever “they” don’t want you to know. Watch everyone toast anyway.

Exit lines (laminate these mentally)

  • “Just kidding — please pass the rolls.”
  • “This is satire. Also, pie.”
  • “I read it on a napkin. Unverified.”
  • “Question everything, especially me. Who wants coffee?”
  • “The Committee has adjourned. Football?”

How to run it as a game

  1. Print the free cards on cardstock. Cut along the dashed lines.
  2. Deal Mild Gravy first. Save Full Tinfoil for after dessert (or never).
  3. Guest reads a card aloud — or whispers it to a co-conspirator for deniability.
  4. First person to get uninvited next year… the Committee is still scoring that.
